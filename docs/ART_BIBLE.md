# Art Bible — Kindlewild (working title; see ORIGINALITY.md)

This is an original setting. It borrows no names, characters, places or lore from any existing game, film or book. Everything here is a proposal for the team to adopt or amend; where shipped content disagrees, see Reconciliation at the end.

## 1. World and premise

**The Kindlewild** is a forest-country whose every living thing once drew warmth and growth from one lantern-tree, the **Heartroot**. Its keeper, the antlered **Old Crown** (currently shipped as "The Warden"), was sworn to keep the Heartroot lit. When the light guttered, the Crown did not die; it hardened, and now guards the dark, striking down anything that carries a flame. The wildlife and the forest's small folk, starved of light, have turned feral and drift toward any glow. You are a **Kindler**: a lamp-bearing scavenger who walks in carrying a scrap of the old fire, and must survive fifteen minutes among things that want it, to reach the Heartroot's stump and relight it.

## 2. Tone and themes

Warm-dark storybook: cosy menace, not gore. Slightly comic (goblins with big eyes and tiny clubs), never grim-dark. Themes: **light versus dark, stewardship gone wrong, scavenger's greed versus duty.** Humour lives in the small things (a fox that is furious, a beetle that is a bee), stakes live in the large ones (the Crown). Violence is puffs, sparks and dissolves; nothing bleeds.

## 3. The place we ship: Verdant Hollow

Biome id `verdant`, displayed "Verdant Hollow". It is the **outer Hollow**, the sunken bowl of forest around the Heartroot's clearing. Fifteen minutes = one long dusk. Once it was the Keeper's orchard: the mossy standing stones are the old boundary markers, the four kinds of shrine are the Keeper's waystations (hearth, path, ward, coin), and the tree stumps are what the Crown felled to starve rivals of shade. Three Crown appearances (5:00, 10:00, 14:30) are the Crown's patrol passing through, each time more awake.

## 4. Ecology and factions (why the cast exists)

Everything is drawn toward light and warmth; the question is what they want from it.

| Current name | Bible role | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| Grunt (goblin fodder) | **Scrapper goblins**, the forest's small folk | Once orchard tenders; feral now. Numerous, cheap, armed with cudgels because tools became clubs. Round, big-eyed = "friendly-looking, still hostile" fodder. |
| Runner (fox) | **Ember foxes** | Fire-eaters who follow flame; fast, single-minded, arrive first. |
| Swarmling (beetle) | **Glimmer beetles** | Hive insects that gather around lamp-light in clouds. Volume, not threat. |
| Stalker (wolf-like) | **Mossfangs** | Pack hunters that track the Kindler by warm scent; flank. |
| Brute (tusked) | **Tuskbacks** | The scrappers' mercenary enforcers; slow, heavy, break lines. |
| Lobber (hooded imp) | **Cinderhexes** | Minor spirits of spent fire; throw spite-sparks. Ranged nuisance, purple-family. |
| Seer (one-eyed robed) | **Hollow Oracles** | Cast-off court augurs of the Crown, still watching for flame with a single eye. Long-range, telegraphed. |
| Tank (stone golem) | **Cairnwights** | The old boundary stones, animated by the Crown's oath. Slow, indestructible-looking walls. |
| Warden (crowned, antlered) | **The Old Crown** | The Keeper itself, an armoured stag-king. Sole boss so far; each visit it has more "awake" cues. |

Factions in one line: the **Small Folk** (goblins, beetles, foxes) are hungry; the **Crown's Court** (oracles, cairnwights, the Crown) is dutiful and hostile; the **Wild** (mossfangs) is neither.

## 5. The hero

The **Kindler** (shipped id `bonker`): young, sturdy, scarf-wearing, a carved **rootclub** in one hand and a hooded **wick-lamp** on the belt whose warm light is the gold pool at their feet. Motivation: the Kindler's village lost its warmth after the Heartroot dimmed; they walk in with a stolen scrap of the old fire to relight it. Alternate heroes (`sliver`, `bastion`, `zip`) are other Kindlers, distinguished by build: the slight one, the armoured one, the quick one. Signature hero colour: warm coral scarf plus lamp gold; **no enemy uses either as a dominant colour.**

## 6. Why shrines, chests, a merchant, and gems/coins

- **Shrines** are the Keeper's waystations, blessings that answer to flame: forge (might), cairn (speed), bulwark (armour), idol (fortune). Sacred = geometric stone, glyph, glowing ring.
- **Chests** are supply caches the small folk hoarded and abandoned; opening one is looting, hence rarer rewards.
- **The merchant** is a **peddler of the Lantern Road**, a neutral wanderer who trades in what the Crown's court dropped. Always hooded, always carrying light, never hostile.
- **XP gems** are **shards of the Heartroot's light** that break out of felled enemies. **Coins** are the small folk's brass tokens. **Hearts/crosses** are sap-heals. In the meta layer, the currency earned on death is a **remnant of the light** (proposed name "motes").

## 7. Naming grammar

Names should sound like one language: **plain English compounds, earthy and warm, a little fairy-tale.**

1. **Creatures**: fodder gets a short single word or blunt compound (Scrapper, Mossfang, Tuskback). Elites are two-part with a material/place prefix (Cairnwight, Hollow Oracle). Bosses take a title plus a place or object ("the Old Crown").
2. **Places**: `[Adjective/nature word] + [terrain]` (Verdant Hollow, Heartroot, Lantern Road, Ashen Barrow).
3. **Items**: material or craft + object (Barkplate, Wick Lens, Brass Bell). Two syllables per word ideal; no sci-fi terms (no "vacuum", "tube", "scrap plating"-style salvage words, no "rocket").
4. **Sacred things** take a virtue or emotion (Rite of Fury, Idol of Avarice, Cairn of Haste): the shrine and its blessing are named with an abstract noun.
5. **Weapons** are folk tools, named as tools (Rootclub, Thornsling, Wisp Ring).
6. **No proper nouns from real IP, no shortened brand-like coinages ("mega", "bonk")**; avoid ALL-CAPS gaming slang. Avoid clashes with famous fantasy/scifi words (Halo, Hulk, Warden of a well-known game, Bastion). Sound rules: hard consonants (k, t, d) for danger, soft (l, m, w) for sacred/friendly; no more than three syllables.
7. **UI voice**: dry, warm, second-person ("Pick a boon", "The Hollow claims you"). No memes.

## 8. Colour language

One meaning per hue. This is the most important rule in the bible.

| Hue | Means | Used on | Never used on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot pink/crimson | **Harm, danger, telegraph**: enemy projectiles, boss bar, damage flash | Enemy shots, boss HP, hit vignette | Pickups, scenery |
| Warm gold / amber | **Reward and value** | Coins, chest, merchant, hero lamp, boss trim (only as "loot-worthy") | Any regular enemy body |
| Cyan-blue | **Growth and hero progress (light)** | XP gems, level-up, XP bar, rare tier, cairn shrine | Enemy bodies (change the goblin) |
| Coral + cream | **The hero** (reserved) | Hero scarf/skin | Anything else |
| Orange-red | **Fire and might** | Forge shrine, fire-eaters (foxes), damage buffs | HP |
| Violet | **Magic/court** (ranged casters, wards) | Cinderhex, Oracle, bulwark shrine | Melee enemies |
| Green | **Nature, heal, uncommon tier** | Ground, heal cross, foliage | Hostile enemies |
| Desaturated dark green-grey | **Scenery** | Ground, trees, stone | Anything the player must act on |
| Neutral stone grey | **Inert/guardian** | Cairnwights, obstacles | Rewards |

Rules: scenery stays below 35% saturation; enemies and rewards may reach 70-90%; **hue is always paired with a shape or a glyph** (colour-blind rule); every enemy body gets one dominant hue plus one accent and no more.

## 9. Shape language

- **Round, big eyes, short limbs** = fodder, fast to read (goblins, beetles).
- **Angular, spiked, horned** = elites and threats (Mossfang spine, Tuskback tusks, Crown antlers/spikes).
- **Tall hooded triangle** = caster or ranged threat (imp, oracle).
- **Blocky, symmetric, wide** = guardian/inert (cairnwight, obelisk, chest).
- **Circle or diamond with core** = pickups; **cross** = heal; **hex/star** = rarity.
- Size ladder (radius in sim units, sprite height): fodder 0.3-0.5, elite 0.5-0.75, heavy 1.0, boss 1.7. Hero sits between fodder and elite at 0.5 but is drawn about 10% larger for presence.

## 10. Line, light and shading

- **Outline**: dark ink (`#05090b`), 2.5-3 px at reference zoom on every actor **and** prop. Outer silhouette outline only, plus inner lines at half weight.
- **Light**: single source, top-left. Cast shadow: flat ellipse under the actor, offset right and down.
- **Shading**: flat cel colour, one shadow tone (about 20% darker, hue shifted slightly toward blue-green) and one highlight tone; no gradients on actors.
- **Rim light**: one thin lighter edge on the top-left contour. The hero alone has a brighter, warm rim.
- **Glow** is reserved for: pickups, shrines, the merchant lamp, projectiles. If it glows it means "interact or avoid".
- **Scenery** uses the same outline but lower contrast and saturation.

## 11. UI style rules

- Panels: deep moss-brown fill, 3 px ink outline, 10-12 px radius, warm brass highlight on the top edge. No neutral black, no hairline borders.
- Type: one chunky display face for titles and numerals, one clean sans for body (bundle both, open licence). No letter-spacing on lower-case text; minimum 13 px.
- Icons: painted in world language (outline, cel shading), 48-64 px, one per item; rarity = frame hue + shape + pips.
- Every important message pairs colour with shape or text.
- HUD positions: vitals top-left, time top-centre, currencies top-right, loadout bottom-centre; do not add a fifth zone.

## 12. Animation principles

1. **Squash and stretch** with a short (80-150 ms) overshoot; weight determines tempo (goblin snappy, cairnwight slow).
2. **Every hit has a reaction**: flash, 2-3 frame hit-stop, recoil. Every kill has a puff in the victim's colour.
3. **Anticipation for danger**: 0.3-0.6 s telegraph (ground ring or wind-up pose) before any damaging move; never damage without telegraph.
4. **Idle life**: everything alive bobs on a distinct tempo; nothing is static.
5. **Reduced-motion mode** removes shake and flashes, never information.

## 13. Expansion rules

New content must: (1) fit an existing ecology role or add one explicitly; (2) take a hue from the table or justify a new one in this document; (3) follow the shape ladder; (4) be named by the grammar; (5) pass the **greyscale silhouette test** (readable as a black shape at 24 px) and the **deuteranopia test**; (6) never resemble a named external character.

**Sketch A: The Ashen Barrow (biome 2).** Burnt-orchard graveyard under grey-violet fog; ground palette ash grey with ember-orange cracks. Cast: **Cindermoths** (swarm, replaces beetles), **Barrow Hounds** (flankers), **Charwights** (ranged, orange), **Sootbell Colossus** (a burnt bell-tower golem heavy), boss: **The Ember Widow** (a spider-priestess of spent fire). Story: where the Crown's first fire went out.

**Sketch B: The Drowned Lantern (biome 3).** Flooded ruin with lily platforms; teal/black water, bioluminescent light. Cast: **Lantern Eels** (ranged, orbit the player), **Reed Ghouls** (slow fodder), **Pearlback Toads** (brutes), **The Undertow Choir** (boss made of three singing shells). Hazard: currents push the player.

**Sketch C: New item class, "Rites".** A passive category replacing "tomes", named for shrine virtues (Rite of Fury, Rite of Haste), drawn as small wax-seal icons in the pink/violet-neutral set so they never compete with pickups.
